## The three categories

A **nutrient** is a vital substance the body needs for growth, energy production and
maintenance of bodily functions. The deck splits them three ways:

| Category | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Macronutrients** | supply energy (calories) | proteins, carbohydrates, fats |
| **Micronutrients** | support functions, needed in small amounts, no calories | vitamins, minerals |
| **Functional components** | add benefits beyond basic nutrition | fibre, polyphenols, probiotics |

Calories come **only** from the three macronutrients. A **kilocalorie** is the heat needed
to raise 1 kg (1 L) of water by 1 °C. Fat gives **9 kcal/g**; carbohydrate and protein 4.

> **Exam focus.** For every nutrient, know its **function, a food source, the deficiency
> and the excess**. That grid is the whole deck.

## Protein

Proteins are chains of **amino acids** that fold into shapes to do specific jobs
(primary → secondary alpha helix/beta sheet → tertiary → quaternary). Some amino acids
the body makes; the **essential** ones must come from food.

- **Functions:** tissue building and repair, **enzymes**, **hormones**, **antibodies**,
  muscle contraction (actin, myosin), transport, fluid balance (osmotic regulation).
- **Sources:** animal (eggs, poultry, fish, dairy); plant (beans, lentils, tofu, quinoa,
  nuts). **Complete sources: eggs, milk, soybean.**
- **Deficiency:** muscle wasting, **edema**, poor wound healing. Severe deficiency in
  children is **kwashiorkor** (edema + growth failure).
- **Excess:** strains the **kidneys** and raises dehydration risk, plus saturated fat from
  heavy meat eating. "High protein without hydration can overload kidney function."

**Supplements:** **whey** is fast-absorbing (pre/post-workout); **casein** is slow-digesting
(before bed); plant proteins come from pea, soy or rice. The protein boom is partly driven
by **GLP-1 drugs** (Ozempic): the weight they remove includes muscle, so users add protein.

## Carbohydrates

The body's **primary energy source**: carbs become **glucose**, which fuels cells,
especially the **brain and red blood cells**. Excess glucose is stored as **glycogen** in
liver and muscle.

| Class (sugar units) | Examples |
|---|---|
| Monosaccharides (1) | glucose, galactose, fructose |
| Disaccharides (2) | sucrose, lactose, maltose |
| Oligosaccharides (3–9) | maltodextrin, raffinose, FOS |
| Polysaccharides (>9) | starch (amylose, amylopectin), glycogen, cellulose |

- **Simple** carbs (white bread, sugar, candy, soft drinks) digest fast; **complex**
  (oats, whole wheat, brown rice, sweet potato, legumes, quinoa) digest slowly.
- **Glycaemic index:** high-GI food (chips, cake, ice cream) spikes and crashes blood sugar,
  giving a higher chronic-disease risk. Low-GI food (lentils, oats, wholegrain bread,
  basmati, oranges) releases slowly.
- **Deficiency:** the low-carb "crash": fatigue plus **hypoglycaemia** (dizziness,
  irritability).
- **Excess** (sugars, refined grains): weight gain and **insulin resistance**, leading to type 2
  diabetes, obesity and CVD.
- **Glucose vs fructose:** glucose is burned or stored as glycogen; **fructose is
  metabolised into fat** in the liver. High-fructose corn syrup is everywhere because it
  sweetens, keeps baked goods soft and extends shelf life.
- **Carb supplements:** maltodextrin/dextrose (fast, intra-workout), **cyclic dextrin**
  (quick, less bloating), oat/sweet-potato powders (slow).

## Fibre

Fibre is a carbohydrate we cannot digest ourselves. It keeps bowel movements regular,
prevents constipation, **lowers cholesterol**, reduces heart-disease risk and **stabilises
blood sugar**. It also acts as a **prebiotic**: gut bacteria ferment it into
**short-chain fatty acids** (butyrate, acetate, propionate).

| Supplement | Note |
|---|---|
| **Psyllium** (Metamucil) | soluble, gel-forming; constipation, cholesterol, blood sugar |
| Methylcellulose (Citrucel) | synthetic, adds bulk, less gas |
| **Inulin** / chicory root | prebiotic, feeds good bacteria, **can bloat** |
| Polycarbophil (FiberCon) | calcium-based tablet |

Who benefits: constipation, diabetes (slows sugar absorption), cholesterol, weight loss
(fullness).

## Fats

Fat gives energy (9 kcal/g), stores energy, builds **cell membranes**, influences
hormones, and is needed to **absorb vitamins A, D, E and K**.

| Type | Structure | Sources | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saturated | no double bond | butter, red meat | **limit** |
| Monounsaturated | one double bond | olive oil, avocado | heart-healthy |
| Polyunsaturated | >1 double bond | nuts, fish (omega-3) | heart-healthy, essential |
| Trans | processed, hydrogenated | processed food | **avoid completely** |

- **Deficiency:** dry skin, hair loss, hormonal imbalance (fat-soluble vitamins not
  absorbed); hinders growth in children.
- **Excess** saturated/trans fat raises **LDL cholesterol**, leading to heart disease,
  **atherosclerosis** (plaque narrowing arteries) and **NAFLD** (fat built up in the liver
  with obesity and insulin resistance).

## Vitamins: fat-soluble vs water-soluble

| | Fat-soluble **A, D, E, K** | Water-soluble **B-group, C** |
|---|---|---|
| Storage | fatty tissue and liver | not stored; must be eaten regularly |
| Excess | accumulates → **hypervitaminosis A, D** | excreted in urine; toxicity rare |

| Vitamin | Main role | Deficiency | Excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | vision (dim light), immunity, skin | **night blindness**, infections | liver damage (UL 3,000 µg retinol; no UL for carotenoids) |
| **D** | calcium absorption, bone, immunity | **rickets** (child), **osteomalacia** (adult) | **hypercalcaemia**: nausea, weakness, kidney damage |
| **E** | antioxidant | rare; nerve damage | **bleeding** (anticoagulant effect) |
| **K** | **blood clotting**, bone | bleeding tendency | interferes with **anticoagulant drugs** |
| **C** | antioxidant, immunity, **collagen** | **scurvy** (bleeding gums, joint pain) | GI upset, diarrhoea |
| B1 thiamine | nerves, carb metabolism | **beriberi** | |
| B3 niacin | metabolism | **pellagra** (3 Ds) | |
| B6 pyridoxine | amino acid metabolism | neuropathy, anaemia | **nerve damage** at high dose |
| B9 folic acid | DNA synthesis, fetal development | **neural tube defects**, anaemia | |
| B12 cobalamin | red blood cells, nerves | **pernicious anaemia**, fatigue | |

Vitamin D comes from sunlight (10–13 min daily), fortified milk, fatty fish, egg yolks and
mushrooms; 66% of Europeans and 75–90% of North Americans are low. Kiwifruit gives about
90 mg of vitamin C per fruit.

## Supplements: what most people actually need

Many **multivitamins are underdosed** (formulated for safety), so the deck suggests
targeting single nutrients instead: **vitamin D3** (take with a fatty meal), **magnesium**
(about half of adults fall short), **B12** (essential for vegans) and **omega-3** (brain
and heart).

## Minerals

| Mineral | Role | Too little | Too much |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcium** (major) | bones, teeth, muscle, nerves | osteoporosis | |
| **Sodium** (major) | fluid balance, nerves, muscle | | **hypertension**, CVD |
| **Potassium** (major) | heart, muscle, blood pressure | **cramps, arrhythmia** | heart danger with kidney disease |
| **Iron** (trace) | haemoglobin, oxygen transport | **anaemia** (fatigue, pale) | organ harm, **hemochromatosis** |
| **Iodine** (trace) | thyroid hormones | **goitre**, hypothyroidism | thyroid dysfunction |
| **Zinc** (trace) | immunity, wound healing, DNA | infections, slow healing, **stunted growth** | nausea, blocks **copper** |

Iron sources: red meat, beans, spinach; menstruation is a common cause of anaemia. Iodine:
iodised salt and seafood (marine animals concentrate it); the RDA rises in pregnancy.
Zinc: meat, seeds, whole grains.

Watch **portion vs serving size**: a Whopper plus large fries is about 1,367 kcal and
1,850 mg sodium. The body is about **62% water**.
