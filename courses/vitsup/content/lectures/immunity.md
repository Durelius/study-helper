## Why immunity matters now

Post-COVID, immunity is a key consumer market (vitamins, probiotics, herbals,
anti-inflammatory foods). Modern life adds **inflammatory pressure**: poor diet,
**ultra-processed food**, pollution, stress (cortisol), sleep deprivation, obesity and
sedentary living. Immunity is now linked to ageing, energy, mental and metabolic health,
not just infection. Class notes add that stress leads to inflammation, which spreads
"to cell, organ, human, society, planet".

## Innate vs adaptive

| | **Innate** | **Adaptive** |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | fast, immediate | slow |
| Specificity | non-specific | targeted to one pathogen |
| Memory | none | **yes**, the basis of vaccination |
| Cells | macrophages, neutrophils | T-cells, B-cells |

**Key cells:**
- **Macrophages** ("Pac-Man") engulf pathogens and start responses.
- **Neutrophils** give rapid defence and are the **most abundant white blood cell** (the class quiz answer).
- **T-cells** execute targeted responses.
- **B-cells** produce **antibodies**.

**Cytokines** are "the chemical language of immunity":
- **pro-inflammatory** (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) trigger fever and pain;
- **anti-inflammatory** (IL-10, IL-4) resolve inflammation;
- **interferons** are antiviral;
- **chemokines** attract immune cells.

Health is a dynamic balance of these, actively resolved by **specialised pro-resolving
mediators (SPMs, resolvins)**.

> **Exam focus.** **Immunity requires balance.** Too weak means infections. Too strong or
> dysregulated means **chronic inflammation or autoimmune disease** (e.g. type 1 diabetes,
> where the immune system keeps destroying the pancreas's insulin cells).

## Acute vs chronic inflammation

Inflammation is a defence triggered by infection, injury, toxins, stress and unhealthy
lifestyles.

- **Acute:** short-term and protective (fever, redness, heat, swelling), self-limiting.
  The steps are PRRs recognising patterns, then cytokines and histamine (vasodilation),
  then chemokines recruiting neutrophils, then resolution by SPMs.
- **Chronic:** **persistent low-grade** activation that becomes damaging. It is the common
  thread of type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, obesity, autoimmune disease,
  **atherosclerosis** (classed as a chronic inflammatory disease), arthritis, IBD,
  depression, Alzheimer's and Parkinson's, and it creates a pro-tumour environment.

**Obesity:** fat tissue is **biologically active**, an endocrine organ secreting TNF-α and
IL-6, which drive insulin resistance in a vicious cycle.

**AGEs (advanced glycation end products):** blood sugar sticks to proteins, producing
reactive oxygen species, inflammation and tissue damage. This is why high glucose inflames
a diabetic's organs.

## Diet: what inflames, what calms

**Pro-inflammatory:** sugar, refined carbs, gluten (per the slide), corn-fed meat and
dairy, **artificial sweeteners**, **trans fats**, highly processed seed oils. Ultra-processed
food carries more salt, sugar and energy and less fibre and micronutrients.

**Anti-inflammatory:** a **Mediterranean-style** diet of vegetables, fruit, omega-3 fatty
fish, fermented foods, fibre, polyphenols, ginger and turmeric.

**Immune nutrients:** vitamin C (antioxidant), **vitamin D** (immune regulation),
**zinc** (immune cell function), **omega-3** (anti-inflammatory), **selenium**
(antioxidant defence), polyphenols and fibre (gut microbiome). **About 70% of immune
activity is associated with the gut.**

## Evidence vs hype

| Supplement | Evidence (slide 34) |
|---|---|
| Vitamin D | **strong, in deficiency states** |
| Vitamin C | strong for **deficiency correction** |
| Zinc | strong for **deficiency correction** |
| Omega-3 | **moderate** for anti-inflammatory effects |
| Probiotics | **strain-specific** immune modulation |
| Curcumin | **promising, needs more robust evidence** |

The lecturer's line: the purpose of supplementing vitamin D, C or zinc is **to fix a
deficiency**, not to "boost" a healthy person.

**Curcumin** is the bioavailability lesson. **Plain extract has about 1% bioavailability**
(500 mg eaten, about 5 mg absorbed: "miserable!!"). Formulation fixes it: **Meriva
phytosome and liposomal curcumin reach about 50×**. In traditional cooking the dose is low
but constant for years.

**PanaNatra (Haleon)** combines curcumin with **boswellic acids** for joint and muscle
pain, sold "from the makers of Panadol" on **trust** in the GSK name.

## COVID-19 lessons

- Severe COVID was driven by **hyperinflammation / cytokine storm**: "the problem wasn't
  solely the virus but the body's excessive inflammatory response".
- People with **obesity and diabetes** already had chronic inflammation, so they faced
  higher risk.
- Buying during COVID (multivitamin 47%, vitamin D 38%, zinc 17%…) was driven by **fear and
  plausibility ahead of evidence**.
- **Vitamin C:** no evidence for megadosing; a 2025 meta-analysis found no significant
  mortality benefit.
- **Zinc:** essential for T-cells, but evidence for megadosing was limited.
- **Vitamin D3:** the **strongest evidence**. Deficiency was not linked to *catching* COVID,
  but severe cases had 64% more deficiency, with more hospitalisation and death.
- Vitamin C **oxidises** (turns brown) in storage, which is a formulation problem.

## The two tiers

**Tier 1, foundational nutrients:**
- **omega-3** (EPA + DHA, which make pro-resolving mediators);
- **vitamin D3 + K2** (an immunomodulator);
- **zinc** (T-cell and NK function);
- **vitamin C** (barrier and neutrophils, depletes during infection);
- **magnesium** (the cofactor for vitamin D activation);
- **quercetin + bromelain** (NF-κB and NLRP3 inhibitor, a natural **zinc ionophore**).

**Tier 2, botanical immunomodulators.** Promising in the lab and in animals, "but in humans
not always":

| Botanical | Key point |
|---|---|
| Curcumin | NF-κB inhibitor, Thai traditional medicine |
| **Andrographis** (Fah Talai Jone) | Thailand's recommended option for mild COVID, **best-selling domestic herbal** |
| **Elderberry** | blocks viral haemagglutinin (cell entry) |
| Astragalus | NK and T-cells, TCM adaptogen |
| **Black ginger** (Kaempferia) | COX-2 and NF-κB, **endemic Thai** |
| Reishi | NK cells and macrophages, Chinese medicine |

## Market cases and consumer psychology

| Case | Lesson |
|---|---|
| **Yakult** | Asia's probiotic success (digestive health, immunity) |
| **Brand's Essence of Chicken** | traditional branding; a systematic review found cognitive effects **at best modest**, so claims are premature |
| **Blackmores "Immunity IQ"** | education campaign, 18M reached, +41% sales: evidence-based messaging beats fear |
| **HymnA** (CJ) | **postbiotic** heat-killed *L. plantarum*; stable, which suits SEA logistics |
| **Tolak Angin** (Indonesian jamu) | "smart people drink" reframing, +65% sales |
| **Enterogermina copycats** | *Bacillus clausii* look-alikes without the original's trials: mimicry, confusion, eroded trust |

Why do consumers prefer pills to lifestyle change? Pills are **convenient**, backed by
**powerful marketing**, and give an **illusion of control** (quick safety, less guilt),
while real change is time-consuming, costly and exhausting.

Trends: **beta-glucans** (prime innate immunity without inflammation), postbiotics and
urolithin A, and NMN/NR (NAD+).
