## The digestive tract

The tract is about **9 metres** long.

| Region | Job |
|---|---|
| Mouth, oesophagus, stomach | chewing, transport, **acid and protein digestion** |
| **Small intestine** (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) | **main site of nutrient absorption** (enterocytes) |
| **Large intestine** | **water reabsorption, bacterial fermentation**, waste |

**Chewing** triggers the whole digestive system and makes you feel full. Class notes
warn that soft ultra-processed food skips those signals, so people keep eating.

The intestinal epithelium is a **selective barrier**: it lets nutrients in and keeps
pathogens out. **Tight junction proteins** seal the cells together. When pathogens or
toxins (e.g. E. coli) break them, you get **"leaky gut"**, linked to inflammation and
metabolic dysfunction.

## The microbiome

About **100 trillion bacteria, roughly 10× the number of human cells**, dominated by
**Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes**, whose ratio affects energy extraction.

| Function | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Metabolic | fermenting indigestible carbs, **making vitamins K and B12**, bile acid and drug metabolism |
| Protective | **competitive exclusion** of pathogens, antimicrobials, barrier integrity |
| Structural | epithelium development, angiogenesis |
| Immunological | **educating the immune system**, regulating inflammation, tolerance |

Babies: breastfed infants carry more **bifidobacteria** (breast milk contains GOS);
bottle-fed infants carry more Bacteroidetes. Class notes on the hygiene idea: animals
raised too clean develop asthma.

**Dysbiosis** is an **imbalance** in the microbiota: fewer good microbes, more harmful ones,
lower diversity. Its causes are **antibiotics**, low-fibre diet, chronic stress, poor sleep
and toxins. It is linked to obesity, type 2 diabetes, IBD, cardiovascular and neurological
disease. Its commonest symptom is **diarrhoea or constipation**:

- **Diarrhoea** comes from infections, food poisoning, intolerance and medications.
- **Constipation** comes from lack of fibre and water, inactivity, medications and thyroid
  problems.

Antibiotics kill good bacteria as well as bad; in Asia, probiotics are often prescribed
alongside them.

**Better digestion:**
- **Do:** eat fibre, stay hydrated, take probiotics and fermented foods, exercise, manage stress.
- **Avoid:** processed food, artificial sweeteners and sugar, refined carbs, high fat,
  alcohol, caffeine, spicy food.

## Pre-, pro-, syn- and postbiotics

| | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Prebiotic** | **fermentable fibre** that selectively feeds good bacteria | inulin (chicory), FOS (banana, onion), **GOS (breast milk)**, **resistant starch** (cooled potato, green banana), **beta-glucan** (oats, barley), pectin (apple) |
| **Probiotic** | **live** microbes that benefit health **in adequate amounts** | *Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium*, *S. boulardii* (yeast), *Akkermansia* |
| **Synbiotic** | probiotic **+** prebiotic | L. paracasei + B. longum + B. breve + inulin/FOS |
| **Postbiotic** | **non-living** microbial metabolites or components | **SCFAs** (acetate, propionate, butyrate), bacteriocins, enzymes, cell-wall parts, heat-killed cells |

> **Exam focus.** One line each: **prebiotic feeds, probiotic lives, postbiotic is what
> they make (or their dead cells).** A probiotic must be **alive, strain-identified, safe
> and proven in humans**, and its benefits are **strain-specific and dose-dependent**.
> Evidence for one strain says nothing about another.

**Dose** is measured in **CFU**, typically **1–100 billion per day**.

**Probiotics vs postbiotics.**
- **Live probiotics** are sensitive to heat, oxygen and pH, often need refrigeration, have
  variable viability, and raise some safety concerns in the immunocompromised.
- **Postbiotics** are **stable at room temperature**, consistent and generally safer, which
  suits Southeast Asian logistics.
- **Spores** (*Bacillus clausii*) survive stomach acid, which is why modern probiotics use them.

**SCFAs** are the colonocytes' main fuel; they are anti-inflammatory and stimulate gut
hormones. Prebiotics raise SCFA production, mineral absorption and barrier function.

## Where the probiotic evidence is

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Antibiotic-associated diarrhoea | risk **about −50%** (S. boulardii, LGG) |
| C. difficile | risk about −60% with antibiotics |
| Infectious diarrhoea | **about 25 hours shorter** |
| IBS | modest but meaningful |
| Ulcerative colitis | **E. coli Nissle 1917** keeps remission; VSL#3 for pouchitis |
| Lactose intolerance, constipation | symptoms improve |

Summary line: **strong evidence for certain GI conditions (AAD, IBS)**, strain-specific,
and not generalisable.

## Obesity, Akkermansia and GLP-1

**Obesity** is a **chronic, relapsing disease**, driven by biology and not just willpower
(BMI ≥30, with lower Asia-Pacific cut-offs). **Metabolic syndrome** is **three or more** of:
abdominal obesity, high triglycerides, low HDL, high blood pressure, high fasting glucose.

**Akkermansia muciniphila** is **low in obesity and diabetes**. Restoring it improves fat
regulation, glucose metabolism and the gut barrier, and stimulates GLP-1.

**Thai synbiotic trial (2021):** a 12-week synbiotic cut weight, BMI, body fat and waist,
and improved lipids, leptin and inflammation.

**GLP-1** is an incretin hormone. It **boosts insulin**, suppresses glucagon, **slows
gastric emptying** and increases satiety.

| Drug | Type | Weight loss |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy; oral Rybelsus) | GLP-1 RA, weekly | about 15–17% |
| Liraglutide (Saxenda, Victoza) | GLP-1 RA, daily | |
| **Tirzepatide** (Mounjaro, Zepbound) | **dual GIP/GLP-1** | about 20–22% |

**Problems:**
- **Weight regain:** about two-thirds of lost weight returns within a year of stopping.
- **Lean muscle loss:** users need protein and resistance training.
- Counterfeit and compounded pens.
- "**Natural GLP-1 boosters**" with misleading claims and modest effects.

## Market and consumer safety

**Thailand** buys both traditional fermented foods (nam pla, khao mak) and modern
probiotics, and **Thai FDA scrutiny of claims** is increasing. Trending ingredients:

- **Strains:** L. gasseri (visceral fat), B. lactis, *Bacillus coagulans*, Akkermansia.
- **Fibres:** inulin, FOS, GOS.
- **Postbiotics:** butyrate, heat-killed lactobacilli.

**Quality problems:** products holding fewer live organisms than labelled, contamination,
misidentified strains, and weight-loss products spiked with **sibutramine** (heart risk),
fenfluramine, laxatives or DNP. Warning signs are miracle promises, rapid loss without
lifestyle change, and **spot reduction**. Protect yourself: look for peer-reviewed trials of
the **specific strain**, **GMP and third-party testing** (NSF, USP, ConsumerLab), reputable
sellers, and FDA warnings. Copycats such as the *B. clausii* look-alikes of Sanofi's
Enterogermina copy the pack but not the evidence.
