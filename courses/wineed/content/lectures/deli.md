## Egg

| Egg | Note |
|---|---|
| Hen | the most used |
| Pheasant | small, thin shell, distinctive smell |
| Guinea fowl | smaller than hen |
| Turkey | bigger than hen; red-yellow-brown shell |
| Duck | flavourful; more yolk |
| Goose | **three times** a hen egg; oily shell |
| Ostrich | 700-800 g; tastes like hen |

White cooks at **60-64 °C** and yolk at **65-70 °C**, so egg-white sauces cook faster. White
digests best cooked or beaten, and yolk lightly cooked, so the **soft-boiled egg** is the
easiest to digest. Soft-boiled is 4-6 minutes from cold water; poached is 3 minutes in water
with vinegar; baked is 6-8 minutes in a water bath. A **frittata** is cooked on both sides;
an **omelette** is folded while still creamy.

**Wine:** boiled eggs take a **dry, young, fresh** white, simple unless there is sauce or
spice. Frittata, omelette or scrambled eggs with meat take a young, dry, fresh, fairly soft
red or white. Fish adds sweet tendency, so a fresh, clear white. **Mushrooms** add aroma, so
a **persistent** wine with quality aromas. Cheese adds flavour, so a **soft** wine.

## Oil, butter, vinegar

- **Olives**: 20-25% oil, 50% water, a little sugar, about 2% other substances (sterols,
  polyphenols, vitamin E, beta-carotene). Olive oil is an antioxidant.
- **Extra virgin olive oil** is triglycerides of monounsaturated **oleic acid**. It sets at
  about 15-16 °C. It aids metabolism, the liver and bile, and controls cholesterol. It
  dresses Mediterranean food and pesto; a bitter oil balances fish and meat; a spicy oil
  suits pizza and game.
- **Other oils:** soybean (like olive, for frying); corn (linoleic and oleic, low-temperature
  cooking); sunflower (mild); peanut (unsaturated, frying); **palm (high smoke point,
  frying)**; coconut (chocolate, cookies, ice cream).
- **Butter**: about **80% fat**, melts at **28-35 °C**, 750 kcal and 250 mg cholesterol per
  100 g. Salted butter has 3-5% salt. The French eat about **9 kg** a year.
- **Margarine**: an emulsion of water and fat made by **hydrogenation** (H2 into oil at
  160-180 °C, nickel or platinum catalyst). Too hot makes **trans fats**. It melts at 40 °C
  and is used in puff pastry.
- **Vinegar**: *Acetobacter* turns wine (5-10% alcohol) into acetic acid. It needs **>6%
  acid and <1.5% alcohol**. **Balsamic**: white grape juice cooked at 70-80 °C, fermented,
  then aged in a **Solera** of small barrels for **at least 12 years**. It comes from
  **Modena and Reggio Emilia** and needs a soft, full, intense, persistent wine.

## Herbs and spices

- **Onion family:** garlic is pungent, used sparingly in the West; shallot is more delicate
  than onion; spring onion is aromatic; **leek** is the most tender and round, for thick
  soups.
- **Leaf herbs:** **basil** (Mediterranean; aromatic when young, spicier when mature);
  parsley (garnish, iron); **sage** (fried in butter as a sauce); mint; oregano (pizza,
  sauces, grills); **rosemary** (grills, masks game); thyme (grills, stews); bay (grills,
  sauces).
- **Spices:** **juniper** de-odours game; **saffron** gives yellow and a sweet scent;
  turmeric gives yellow with less scent; black pepper is pungent, pink pepper aromatic;
  **paprika** is two chillies blended, used in Eastern Europe; **nutmeg** and **clove** come
  from the **Moluccas** (Indonesia); **cinnamon** from **Sri Lanka**; **star anise** from
  **China and Vietnam**; **ginger** from China; cardamom (ginger, lemon, eucalyptus) goes in
  Nordic bread and South Asian curry; **vanilla** from Madagascar, Java and the Seychelles;
  **mustard** makes sauces in France, England and Italy.

## Pasta, rice, vegetables

**Dry pasta** is durum wheat and water: kneaded, shaped, dried. **Fresh pasta** adds hen
eggs, must be kept cold and does not last. **Pizza** was born in **Naples in 1830**. It has a
sweet tendency from the base. Beer is its best partner, or a light, dry or sparkling white;
salami pizza takes a refreshing red.

Rice is classified by grain size and starch/protein. Round, starchy grains break easily and
go in soup. **Carnaroli and Arborio** make **risotto**. **Jasmine and Basmati** are long and
suit fish, meat or spiced food. **Black rice** cooks longest and needs a sauce.

**Wine:** pasta with oil or butter and parmesan takes a moderately soft, fresh, light white.
**Tomato and basil** take a soft, medium, fresh white or a **rosé**. Seafood adds sweet
tendency, so a **fresh** white. **Spiced rice and curry** need high aromatic stability.

| Vegetable tendency | Examples |
|---|---|
| Sweet | tomato, zucchini, pumpkin flower, asparagus, spinach, cauliflower, carrot, onion, potato |
| Bitter | salad, spinach, celery, asparagus |
| **Sour** | **tomato** |
| Aromatic | tomato, aubergine, cucumber, asparagus, celery, onion |

Beans are **30-50% starch** (sweet tendency); fresh winged beans are bitter too; fava beans
are fragrant. **Truffle** is about 10% water and sugar and 6% protein; its value is its
**hydrocarbon** aroma. **White truffle** is sweeter-smelling and **dearer** than black.

**Wine:** a sweet salad takes a light, fairly soft white. Rosemary potatoes take a fragrant
**Riesling**. Baked zucchini, onion and pepper with cheese take a fresh, rich white (Kerner,
Alto Adige) or a light-tannin red (Pinot Noir). Lentil soup with beef takes a fresh red with
some tannin. **Bitter vegetables need a very smooth (soft) wine.** Sour tomato needs a soft
wine. Truffle needs an elegant wine.

## Meat and cured meat

| Colour | Meats |
|---|---|
| **White** | veal, rabbit, lamb, goat, chicken, turkey |
| **Red** | beef, pork, goat, horse, buffalo |
| **Black** | **game** |

Colour comes from **myoglobin**, rich in iron. Only **female pheasant** is usually eaten.
Northern Europe cooks game with spice and fruit; Southern Europe uses only spices and herbs.
**Foie gras** is an enlarged goose or duck liver, with sweet tendency, tenderness and
bitterness; on bread it takes **Sauternes, Barsac or Muffa Nobile**. **Pâté** is liver with
30-50% other meat; Supreme, Timbal and Lingot mean more liver and a higher price.

**Cooking and wine:** grilled meat (charred skin, bitter) takes a soft wine with tannin.
Fried meat takes a fresh wine with moderate tannin. Roast beef or steak takes a red with
moderate alcohol and tannin and some freshness. Simmered beef takes a soft red. Duck and
pheasant take a very soft wine. **Game takes a red with very high body, intensity and long
aroma.**

**Cured meats** are 25-50% water, 25-30% fat and protein.

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Raw, minced | pork or wild boar sausage |
| Raw, whole muscle | **Bresaola (beef)**, **Prosciutto (pork)** |
| Raw, muscle with fat | **Pancetta (belly)**, **Guanciale (cheek)** |
| Cooked | Mortadella, Würstel |

Parma ham takes a flavourful, fresh, soft, persistent white. **Salty** cured meat takes a
soft wine, never a bitter one. **Smoked** takes a soft red with moderate tannin, not a big
body. **Fatty** cured meat takes **sparkling** wine.

## Seafood

Fish fillets are 18-20% protein. Fat: **low <3%**, medium 3-10%, **high >10%**. Many sea fish
are rich in omega-3. **Crustaceans** (shrimp, crayfish, crab, lobster) are **low-fat (0.5-2%)
but very high in cholesterol (80-150 mg/100 g)**; lobster suits an elegant sparkling wine.
**Molluscs** include single-shell babylon, clams, cockles, oysters, calamari, cuttlefish and
octopus. **Oysters** are rich in zinc; the best are Atlantic (Brittany, Normandy): **Belon,
Marennes**. **Caviar** comes from **sturgeon**: Beluga, Ship, Osetra, Sevruga.

**Wine:** sashimi takes a fresh white or sparkling. **Oysters take a soft sparkling.**
Caviar takes a soft, high-alcohol wine (vodka in Russia). Steamed seafood takes a fresh,
flavourful, aromatic white. **Fried** seafood takes a **high-body, high-alcohol** white.
**Eel** is hard to match: try a **sparkling red**.

## Cheese

Cheese is made from cow, buffalo or goat milk; goat tends to be **sour**. Mould, bacteria and
**rennet** curdle the milk. The curd is cooked, drained and shaped, and salt goes in before
ageing.

| Family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Acid-coagulated | cottage, cream, quark, queso blanco |
| Heat/acid | ricotta |
| Extra-hard | **Grana Padano, Parmesan**, Asiago, Sbrinz |
| Hard / semi-hard | Cheddar, Cheshire / Caerphilly, Mahón, Monterey Jack |
| With eyes | Swiss-type (**Emmental**, Gruyère); Dutch-type (Edam, Gouda) |
| High salt | Domiati, **Feta** |
| Pasta filata | **Mozzarella**, Kashkaval, Provolone |
| Surface mould | **Brie, Camembert** |
| Internal mould (blue) | **Roquefort, Gorgonzola, Stilton**, Danablu |
| Surface-ripened | Limburger, Munster, Taleggio, Port Salut |

**Wine:** Mozzarella (milky, sour tendency) takes a soft, flavourful white or sparkling.
Short-aged soft cheese takes a soft Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc. **Brie and Camembert take
soft whites.** **Hard, intense cheeses** (Pecorino Romano, Grana Padano, Parmigiano) take an
**evolved, bottle-aged red** or a smooth sparkling. **Blue cheeses** are very salty and take a
**very full red or a soft sweet wine**.
