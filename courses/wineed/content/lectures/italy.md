## History and legislation in brief

The Greeks and Etruscans planted the first vines around **400 BC**, and the Romans spread
viticulture. The **first Super Tuscan** appeared in the **1960s** (Tenuta San Guido). Italy is
now the world's largest producer and exporter.

The four levels:

- **DOCG**: the highest; strict control and a quality guarantee.
- **DOC**: the largest; medium quality, though some are as good as DOCG.
- **IGT**: quality wine outside the DOC rules.
- **VdT**: no specification.

On the label, **Classico** marks the historic zone, **Riserva** longer barrel ageing, and
**Superiore** at least 0.5% more alcohol.

## North-West: Piemonte, Lombardia, Liguria

- **Piemonte**: **Barolo DOCG** and **Barbaresco DOCG**, both **Nebbiolo**. Barbaresco is less
  tannic, with redder fruit, and its perfume develops faster. Barbera gives easy-drinking reds;
  Moscato makes sparkling wine by **Metodo Asti**: one fermentation in a sealed tank, stopped early
  to keep sugar. Valle d'Aosta and Piemonte also grow fine Chardonnay.
- **Lombardia**: **Sforzato di Valtellina DOCG** (dried Nebbiolo) and **Franciacorta DOCG**,
  Metodo Classico from Chardonnay 80%, Pinot Nero 15% and Pinot Bianco 5%. *Satèn* is softer.
  Garda DOC and Lugana DOC come from southern Lake Garda.
- **Liguria**: **Riviera Ligure di Ponente DOC** (Pigato, which smells of basil and pairs with
  pesto; Vermentino) and sweet **Sciacchetrà DOC**.

## North-East: Trentino-Alto Adige, Veneto, FVG, Emilia-Romagna

| Region | Designations and grapes |
|---|---|
| **Trentino-Alto Adige** | Gewürztraminer originates in Alto Adige; **Trento DOC** (Metodo Classico); **Teroldego Rotaliano DOC** |
| **Veneto** | **Amarone della Valpolicella DOCG** (dried Corvina 75%, Rondinella 20%, Molinara 5%); **Conegliano Valdobbiadene Prosecco DOCG** (**Glera**; Cartizze); **Soave Superiore DOCG** (**Garganega**) |

| **Emilia-Romagna** | **Lambrusco di Sorbara DOC** (slightly sparkling, sweet red, with tortellini); Colli Bolognesi Pignoletto DOCG; **Romagna Albana DOCG** (sweet white) |

## Central Italy

- **Toscana**: **Sangiovese** is the star.
  - **Chianti Classico DOCG** needs at least 80% Sangiovese and 12% alcohol. Riserva needs 24
    months and 12.5%; Gran Selezione has existed since 2014.
  - **Brunello di Montalcino DOCG** is very expensive. **Vino Nobile di Montepulciano DOCG** and
    **Carmignano DOCG** are also Sangiovese-based.
  - **Bolgheri DOC** and Maremma Toscana DOC make Bordeaux-style blends on sandy-clay soil.
    **Bolgheri Sassicaia DOC** is the only designation tied to one producer. Tignanello and
    Solaia are *Rosso IGT*.
- **Umbria**: **Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG**, tannic and long-lived, good with game.
- **Lazio**: **Cesanese del Piglio DOCG**; Shiraz grows well (pair it with carbonara).
- **Marche and Abruzzo**:
  - **Verdicchio** (Castelli di Jesi) is a white with body and longevity.
  - The **Montepulciano** grape makes **Conero DOCG** and **Montepulciano d'Abruzzo Colline
    Teramane DOCG**, plus Abruzzo rosé.
  - **Molise DOC** is also made from Montepulciano.

> **Exam focus.** *Vino Nobile di Montepulciano* is named after a Tuscan **town** and is made
> from Sangiovese. *Montepulciano d'Abruzzo* is named after the **grape**.

## The South and the islands

| Region | Designations and grapes |
|---|---|
| **Campania** | **Taurasi DOCG** (Aglianico), **Fiano di Avellino DOCG**, **Greco di Tufo DOCG**; Asprinio sparkling |
| **Basilicata** | **Aglianico del Vulture DOC** |
| **Puglia** | **Primitivo di Manduria DOC** (Primitivo = Zinfandel); Negroamaro |
| **Calabria** | Gaglioppo |
| **Sicilia** | the most wine in Italy. **Etna DOC** (Nerello Mascalese, like Bourgogne); Nero d'Avola; **Cerasuolo di Vittoria DOCG** (Frappato); **Passito di Pantelleria DOC**; **Marsala** (fortified, Trapani). Catarratto is Italy's most-produced white grape |
| **Sardegna** | **Vermentino di Gallura DOCG**; Cannonau = Grenache, Carignano = Carignan |
