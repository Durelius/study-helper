## South Africa

Jan van Riebeeck planted the first vines at the Cape in **1655**; Simon van der Stel made wine
in Stellenbosch (1679) and Constantia (1685). The **KWV** (1957) set quotas until 1997. South
Africa is now a top-10 producer. Its style is said to marry Old World structure with New
World fruit.

- **Chenin Blanc** is the most planted grape, historically used for distilling.
- **Pinotage** = **Pinot Noir × Cinsaut**, bred by **Abraham Perold in 1924** and commercial
  from 1961. It has plum and raspberry fruit and good tannin. Blends with it are called a
  **Cape Blend**.
- **Méthode Cap Classique (MCC)** is the Traditional Method sparkling.
- **Wine of Origin (WO)**: Region › District › Ward.

| Region | Note |
|---|---|
| **Stellenbosch** | the key region; **False Bay** moderates it; research institutes |
| Paarl | warm; from sparkling to fortified |
| **Constantia** | humid; **botrytis Sauvignon Blanc** |
| **Swartland** | Rhône-style reds |
| Darling | Benguela current; Sauvignon Blanc |
| **Klein Karoo** | Port-style, like the Douro |
| Worcester / Robertson | brandy from Chenin and Colombard / Chardonnay sparkling |
| Elgin, Walker Bay | cool; Sauvignon Blanc; Burgundy-style |

## USA

Spanish missionaries planted the first vines (18th century). Then came phylloxera and
**Prohibition, 1920-1933**. **Robert Mondavi** built his Napa winery in **1966**. Pinot Noir
went to Oregon in the 1970s. The designation is the **AVA** (American Viticultural Area),
which zones by region only.

California is the largest area. **Cabernet Sauvignon** is the most planted red, then
**Zinfandel** and Merlot; **Chardonnay** is the most planted white. **Zinfandel = Primitivo**,
originally from Croatia and Puglia and brought by Italian immigrants. It ripens unevenly;
**White Zinfandel** (blush) is a sweet pink wine invented in California.

| Area | Grape |
|---|---|
| **Napa Valley** (Rutherford, Oakville, St Helena, Stags Leap…) | **Cabernet Sauvignon** |
| **Carneros** | Chardonnay, Pinot Noir (Pacific breeze) |
| Sonoma: Russian River, Sonoma Coast | Pinot Noir, Chardonnay (cool, fog) |
| Sonoma: Alexander Valley; Dry Creek | Cabernet, Merlot; **Zinfandel** |
| Mendocino | Zinfandel; McDowell Valley Shiraz |
| Santa Rita Hills / Santa Maria | Pinot Noir / Chardonnay |
| Paso Robles | Zinfandel, Cabernet, Shiraz |
| **Washington: Columbia Valley** | Riesling; Merlot more intense than Bordeaux |
| **Oregon: Willamette Valley** | **Pinot Noir** |
| New York: Finger Lakes; Long Island | Riesling, Gewürztraminer; Bordeaux-style reds |

## Chile and Argentina

**Chile.** The Spanish brought vines in the 16th century and the French built the industry in
the 19th. It is **phylloxera-free**, walled in by the Andes, the Atacama Desert and the cold
south. **Carménère** (identified in **1994**) is Bordeaux's lost **Grand Vidure**; it is high
in alcohol with chocolate notes. **Cabernet Sauvignon** is the most planted.

| Valley | Note |
|---|---|
| Limarí (Coquimbo) | hot, dry, Pacific-cooled |
| Aconcagua | warm, dry; reds |
| **Casablanca** | **coolest**; Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir |
| San Antonio / **Leyda** | **Humboldt current**; cool |
| **Maipo** | the industry's centre; Cabernet with **blackcurrant, eucalyptus** |
| Rapel: Cachapoal, **Colchagua** | modern wine tourism; intense reds |
| Maule | País; Carménère at San Clemente |
| Itata, Bío-Bío, Malleco | old País and Moscatel; wettest; new and cool |

**Argentina.** Wine came with 19th-century European immigrants; the railway linked Buenos
Aires to **Mendoza**, which makes **about 70%** of the country's wine. **Malbec** was planted by
the French and does better than in France thanks to the sun. **Torrontés** is a white,
believed to be from Galicia. Reds are full and extracted.

| Region | Note |
|---|---|
| **Uco Valley** | Andes foothills, 900-1,500 m |
| Upper Mendoza River | 1,000 m; aromatic, high-acid Malbec |
| **Salta** | up to 2,200 m; **90% Torrontés**; also Tannat |
| La Rioja, San Juan, Río Negro | hot and dry; raisins; new |

## Australia and New Zealand

**Australia.** Arthur Phillip planted the first vines (Sydney, 1788). **James Busby**
collected 543 varieties. Phylloxera hit **Victoria (1875)** but not South Australia.
Regions have been **Geographic Indications (GI)** since **1994**.

**Shiraz** is the most planted (Barossa, McLaren Vale: ripe, alcoholic, oaky). Cabernet
Sauvignon is second (Coonawarra, Margaret River). **Chardonnay** is the top white.

| Region | Signature |
|---|---|
| **Barossa Valley** | heart of the industry; **Shiraz** |
| Eden Valley, **Clare Valley** | **Riesling** |
| **Coonawarra** | **terra rossa**; Bordeaux-style **Cabernet** |
| McLaren Vale, Langhorne Creek | Shiraz; Cabernet-Shiraz |
| **Hunter Valley** (NSW) | **Sémillon** ageing 15-20 years |
| Tumbarumba; Yarra Valley | Champagne-style sparkling; Chardonnay and Pinot |
| **Tasmania** | **coldest**, most maritime |
| **Margaret River** | like Bordeaux's **Right Bank** |

**New Zealand.** Samuel Marsden planted the first vines (Kerikeri, 1819); the cold South
Island came into production in the 1970s. **Sauvignon Blanc** is **42%** of plantings, ahead of
Chardonnay; Pinot Noir is growing in the south.

| Region | Signature |
|---|---|
| **Marlborough** (from 1973) | over half of producers; **Sauvignon Blanc**; Cloudy Bay |
| **Hawke's Bay** | warm; **Bordeaux blends** |
| Wellington & Wairarapa | over half the Pinot Noir area |
| Gisborne | Chardonnay, Gewürztraminer |
| **Central Otago** | cold; delicate, expensive **Pinot Noir** |
