## Spain

Phoenicians and Greeks brought vines to **Andalucía** (1,100-500 BC). Sherry was traded from
the 14th century. **Rioja** arose in the 1850s using Bordeaux methods. Spain joined the EU in
1986 and is now the **third** largest producer, after Italy and France.

**Classification, high to low:** **DO Pago** › **DOCa** (quality kept >10 years) › **DO**
(equal to France's AOC) › VCIG (2003) › Vino de la Tierra › Vino de Mesa.

| Region | Grape | Wines |
|---|---|---|
| Galicia | **Albariño**, Godello | **Rías Baixas** |
| Castilla y León (largest) | **Tempranillo**; **Verdejo**, Sauvignon Blanc | **Ribera del Duero** (Vega Sicilia), **Rueda** |
| País Vasco | Hondarribi Zuri | Txakoli |
| La Rioja | Tempranillo, Garnacha, Cariñena (Mazuelo), Graziano | **Rioja DOCa** |
| Aragón | Cariñena | **DO Cariñena**, the first DO (1960) |
| Catalunya (most diverse) | Garnacha, Cariñena; Macabeo, Parellada, Xarel-lo | **Cava**, **Priorat DOCa** |
| Castilla-La Mancha | Tempranillo; **Airén** | La Mancha, Valdepeñas |
| Valencia | Monastrell; Merseguera; Moscatel | Alicante, Moscatel de Valencia |
| Andalucía | **Palomino**, Pedro Ximénez | **Jerez (Sherry)**, Montilla-Moriles, Málaga |
| Canaries | Listán Negro, Malvasía | Tacoronte-Acentejo, Lanzarote |

**Rioja** comes from three zones: Alta, Alavesa and Baja. The blend is about 70%
Tempranillo (freshness, fruit), 20% Garnacha (**weight, alcohol**), 10% Cariñena (**colour,
softness**) and a little Graziano (delicacy). It has strawberry, raspberry and oak notes.
Ageing: **Crianza 12 + 12 months**, **Reserva 36 months (≥12 in barrel)**, **Gran Reserva 24
in barrel + 36 in bottle**. The glossary slide gives generic Spanish figures that differ:
Crianza 6 + 18, Reserva 12 + 3 years, Gran Reserva 15 months + 5 years. Use the Rioja
figures for Rioja.

**Cava**: Traditional Method since 1860, the second-best-selling sparkling after Champagne.
About 90% comes from Sant Sadurní d'Anoia (Penedès). Parellada gives mellowness, Macabeo
freshness, Xarel-lo body.

## Sherry

Palomino on chalky soil. Fortify to **14.5%** so **flor**, a yeast film, grows: it keeps air
out and gives a nutty scent. Ageing is by **solera** (five barrels; each year ⅔ of the wine
is mixed with ⅓ younger wine). Heavy flor goes to the **Fino** solera; light flor to
**Oloroso**.

| Style | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Fino** | the palest; aged by the cool sea it becomes **Manzanilla** (orange zest, almond); sweet = Pale Cream |
| **Amontillado** | flor dies after about 5 years (Fino-amontillado), Amontillado after 10; dark, pungent, soft; sweet = **Medium** |
| **Oloroso** | solera >10 years; rich; sweetened with PX = **Cream** |
| **Palo Cortado** | flor dies **by accident**; Fino + Oloroso character; rare |
| Varietal, Age-dated, Vintage | Moscatel or PX; average age 12/15/20/30; single year, no solera |

## Portugal

The Phoenicians, then Rome. Trade with Britain began in **1386**, and Port and Madeira went
to Britain in the 18th century. Salazar favoured wheat over vines until 1974. EU entry came
in 1986.

**Classification:** **DOC** › IPR › Vinho Regional › Vinho de Mesa. Label words: Quinta
(estate), Colheita (harvest year), Garrafeira (aged under tighter rules than Reserva),
Reserva (+0.5% alcohol), Verde (young).

| DOC | Grape / style |
|---|---|
| **Vinho Verde** | largest DOC; young white, 8.5-11.5%, slight fizz |
| **Porto / Douro** | Port; Douro reds from **Touriga Nacional** |
| **Dão** | Touriga Nacional, floral |
| **Bairrada** | **Baga**, intense, long-lived |
| Colares | sea cliffs, free of phylloxera |
| **Bucelas** | **Arinto** (lemony white) |
| Ribatejo | Fernão Pires whites |
| **Setúbal** | **Moscatel de Setúbal** (fortified, with Christmas pudding) |
| Alentejo | largest province, cork; Aragonez, Trincadeira; Antão Vaz, Roupeiro |
| **Madeira** | volcanic, steep *poios*; noble **Sercial, Verdelho, Bual, Malvasia**; Tinta Negra Mole most planted |

**Port**: fermentation is cut after about **48 h** with grape spirit, leaving sugar. Grapes:
Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz, Tinta Barroca, Touriga Nacional, Tinta Cão.

| Port | Rule |
|---|---|
| Ruby | young blend, 2-3 years |
| Tawny | longer than Ruby, softer |
| **Vintage** | one year; 2 years in tank, 20 in bottle, unfiltered |
| **LBV** | one good year; 4-6 years in barrel, filtered |
| **Colheita** | one year; at least 7 years in barrel |
| Crusted | vintage-like but several years |
| White | white grapes; less sweet; aperitif |

**Madeira**: fortified with 95% spirit, then heated, moving from hot upper shelves to cooler
ones. Cheap Madeira goes in **estufas at 50 °C for 90+ days**. It is among the longest-lived
wines.

## Germany, Austria, Switzerland

**Germany.** Charlemagne planted the Rheingau. **Riesling** is the key grape (Mosel, Rhein;
botrytis). **Spätburgunder** = Pinot Noir, Weissburgunder = Pinot Blanc. Whites are made in
stainless steel, so no oak masks the terroir.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **QmP** (highest) | quality wine by ripeness: Kabinett (medium dry) › **Spätlese** (late harvest) › Auslese › **Beerenauslese** (mostly botrytised) › **Eiswein** (frozen grapes) › **TBA** (only botrytised) |
| QbA | chaptalisation allowed |
| Landwein / Deutscher Tafelwein | like Vin de Pays / table wine |

Label words: Trocken (dry), Halbtrocken, **Sekt** (sparkling), Weingut (winery), Einzellage
(single vineyard).

Regions: **Mosel** (cool, slightly sweet Riesling); **Ahr** (very northern, Spätburgunder);
**Rheingau** (dry, mineral Riesling); **Rheinhessen** (largest); Pfalz (warm, dry Riesling);
Baden (south; Müller-Thurgau, Pinots, Gutedel); **Württemberg** (over 70% red: Trollinger,
Schwarzriesling, Spätburgunder); Franken (Silvaner).

**Austria.** Celts, then monks. The Klosterneuburg wine school dates from 1860. The **1985
chemical scandal** hurt its name; it has recovered in the last 20 years.

| Grape | Note |
|---|---|
| **Grüner Veltliner** | herbs, white pepper, grapefruit; best from **Wachau** |
| Welschriesling | **not** related to Riesling; light, citrus, for Sekt |
| Zierfandler + Rotgipfler | blended into Gumpoldskirchner |
| Neuburger | Weissburgunder × Silvaner; nutty |
| **Blaufränkisch** | dark, raspberry; Burgenland |
| **Zweigelt** | St. Laurent × Blaufränkisch; cherry |
| **Blauer Wildbacher** | Steiermark; rosé **Schilcher** |

Regions: Niederösterreich (Grüner, Riesling, Danube); **Wachau** (granite); **Burgenland**
(reds, and botrytis from **Lake Neusiedl**); Steiermark (aromatic whites); Wien.

**Switzerland.** Drinks more than it makes; exports are **under 1%**. Half red, half white.
The French-speaking west (Valais is the biggest, plus Vaud, Genève, Neuchâtel) grows
**Chasselas** and native Amigne, Petite Arvine, Cornalin and Humagne Rouge. The German-speaking
east makes the best **Blauburgunder**; Thurgau is the birthplace of **Müller-Thurgau** (Dr.
Hermann Müller). The Italian-speaking south grows **Merlot**, and Bondola makes **Nostrano**.

## Eastern Europe

**Hungary.** Native grapes are Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch), Olaszrizling (Welschriesling) and
**Furmint**. **Eger**: **Bikavér** ("Bull's Blood", Kadarka-based). **Szekszárd**: the most
red, paprika-scented. **Somló**: full whites (Furmint, Hárslevelű, **Juhfark**).

**Tokaji**, which Louis XIV called "wine of kings, king of wines", is made from **Furmint**,
**Hárslevelű** and Muscat (Muskotály) with botrytis. **Aszú** is graded **3-6 puttonyos**.
**Eszencia** is the free-run syrup, fermented apart. It ages in cellars lined with the black
mould *Racodium cellare*. Other styles: **Szamorodni** (whole bunches), **Fordítás**
(re-soaked on skins), **Máslás** (on Aszú lees).

| Country | Signature |
|---|---|
| **Romania** | Latin among Slavs; **Fetească Neagră** (red), Fetească Albă (white); Moldova is the largest region; Murfatlar makes late-harvest sweet wine |
| **Bulgaria** | founded 681; Mavrud, Melnik, Rubin, Gamza; Misket, Dimiat; **Mavrud** south of Plovdiv; **Melnik** by the Struma |
| Czech Republic | Bohemia: **Riesling**; Moravia: **Grüner Veltliner**, Müller-Thurgau |
| Slovakia | Malokarpatská; Frankovka, St. Laurent; Tokajská |
| Slovenia | Podravje, Posavje, Primorje; Maribor has a 400-year-old vine; **Refošk** |
| **Croatia** | **Plavac Mali** (Zinfandel-like); Pošip, Grk, Maraština; **Graševina** (Welschriesling) |

## Greece and Eurasia

**Greece**: over 4,000 years of wine and the **amphora**. About 70% of its wine is white.
Over 300 native grapes. Reds: **Xinomavro** (Macedonia; rich, tannic, long-lived),
**Aghiorghitiko**. Whites: **Assyrtiko** (Santorini, mineral), Roditis, **Savatiano**
(**Retsina**, flavoured with pine resin), Moschofilero, Debina (Epirus). Islands: Rhodes
(Athiri; Muscat), Samos and Limnos (sweet Muscat), Crete (Vilana; Kotsifali + Mandelaria).

| Country | Signature |
|---|---|
| **Cyprus** | Troodos vineyards up to 1,500 m; **Commandaria** sweet wine (800 BC); Mavro, **Xynisteri** |
| **Lebanon** | about 6,000 years; **Bekaa Valley** is its only zone; native **Obaideh, Merwah**; French influence |
| **Israel** | **Baron Edmond de Rothschild**, 1880s; international grapes; Galilee, Samaria, Judean Hills |
| **Turkey** | believed origin of *Vitis vinifera*; industry from the 1920s; Thrace-Marmara; Anatolia **Boğazkere** |
