## History of beer

Beer goes back to about **4,000 BC**, and hops came in about 1,000 years later. The
**Sumerians** in Mesopotamia brewed first, then Egypt. The Etruscans drank it, and the Romans
and Greeks called it **"malt wine"**. Roman breweries stood in Regensburg and Trier, and the
brewer was the *cervesarius*.

Medieval **monks** (Benedictine, Cistercian, Franciscan) were the experts. There were about
300 monastery breweries before the 1830 secularisation; about 20 may still say
*Klosterbrauerei*. Monks introduced **hops** for bitterness and as an antiseptic against
lactic bacteria.

In **1516 William IV of Bavaria** allowed only **malt, hops and water**, for health and to cut
imports. The law holds only in Bavaria. In **1842 Josef Groll** made the first **Pilsner** at
Pilsner Urquell (Bohemia): cool fermentation, light malt, soft water, Bohemian hops.

## Raw materials

- **Cereals.** Starch needs the enzyme **diastase** to become **maltose** (fermentable, into
  glucose) and **dextrin** (not fermentable, it gives **body and foam**). **Barley** is the
  main cereal, and **two-row** barley is best. It is used malted (fresh barley tastes bad),
  and roasted for stout. About 15 kg of barley makes a hectolitre.
- **Other cereals.** Rice gives **dryness**, maize and oats give **softness**, millet gives a
  light body. Belgium adds **sucre candi** (caramelised beet sugar) to raise alcohol without
  a malt taste.
- **Hops.** **Unfertilised female flowers**, rich in **lupulin** (resins and oils). Resins
  give **bitterness**, are antibacterial and help foam last. Oils give perfume. Tannins clarify
  by coagulating protein. Only **1/5** of the tannin bitterness is from hops; **4/5 is from the
  barley husk**. Aroma hops: **Cascade, Fuggle, Hallertau Mittelfrueh, Tettnang, Spalt,
  Saaz**. Cheap, high-yield hops: Northern Brewer, Brewer Gold, Hallertau Magnum. **England
  dry-hops** during ageing.
- **Water.** Hardness in French degrees: soft ≤10°F, medium 10-20°F, hard >20°F. Light beer
  needs **soft** water; dark beer can use hard. Reverse osmosis sets any profile.
- **Yeast.** *S. carlsbergensis* (Hansen, Carlsberg, 1882) works **low, 5-10 °C**, and settles
  at the **bottom**: lager. *S. cerevisiae* works **high, 15-25 °C**, and rises to the
  **top**: ale.

## Production

1. **Malting.** Soak at 10-15 °C for 48 h (moisture 14 → 45%), germinate 6 days at 14-16 °C, then kiln for 24 h down to 5%. Pilsen malt is kilned at **85 °C**; dark malt at 100-250 °C, where **melanoidins** give colour and aroma.
2. **Wort.** Mash at 35-75 °C, finishing at 76-78 °C. Filter, then **boil with hops**: this extracts bitterness, coagulates protein and stops the enzymes. Filter again and cool.
3. **Fermentation.** About 1 week (10 °C low, 20 °C high). The CO2 is recaptured. **Maturation** at about **0 °C for 4-6 weeks** at 0.5 bar.
4. **Bottling.** Clean, fill without losing gas, cap, **pasteurise**, label. Kegs and cans protect from light. Bottles and cans: **60 °C water for 15 min**; kegs: **70 °C for seconds**.

## Classification and styles

Classification uses **alcohol %** and **saccharometric degree** (°Plato); alcohol is about 40%
of °P.

| Type | °P | Alcohol |
|---|---|---|
| Non-alcoholic | 3-8 | <0.8% |
| Light | 5-10.5 | 1.2-3.5% |
| Beer | >10.5 | >3.5% |
| Special | >12.5 | >5% |
| Double malt | >14.5 | >6% |

A litre of beer is about 90% water, 5% alcohol, 4% carbohydrate and 450 kcal.

**By fermentation:** **lager** (low temperature) is the most made and drunk. **High
fermentation** is 10% of production but 50% of consumption in Bavaria and Britain.
**Spontaneous** fermentation (wild yeasts and lactic bacteria) is Belgian: **Lambic, Gueuze**,
about 1% of the market, around Brussels and the Senne valley.

| Style | Origin | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Pils** | Bohemia | origin of modern light beer; pale, dry, hoppy |
| **Bock / Doppelbock** | Einbeck | >7%; Doppelbock is the strongest Bock |
| Märzen, Münchner | Munich | Münchner was Munich's best-seller in the 19th century |
| **Rauch** | Bamberg | smoked: malt dried over a direct flame |
| Vienna | Vienna | dark amber, caramel |
| Dry | Japan | pale, dry |
| Ice | USA | made by undercooling a lager |
| **Alt** / **Kölsch** | Düsseldorf / Cologne | high fermentation |
| **Weizen**, Kristallweizen | Bavaria | wheat malt, fruity |
| Berliner Weisse | Berlin | very acidic; with syrup |
| **IPA** | Britain | the strongest **Pale Ale** |
| **Brown Ale** | England | the strongest **Mild Ale** |
| Barley Wine | Britain | about 12%, vinous |
| **Stout** | Ireland | dark, very bitter, dense foam |
| **Trappist** / Abbey | Belgium / NL | Trappist still brewed by monks; Abbey now commercial; 9-10% |
| Blanche | Belgium | citrus, spice; refermented in bottle |
| Saison | Wallonia | sold at harvest time |
| **Lambic** | Brussels | spontaneous; aged in bottle |
| **Gueuze** | Belgium | old + young Lambic |
| **Kriek / Frambozen / Faro** | Belgium | Lambic + **cherry** / **raspberry** / sugar |

## Tasting, serving and food

**Visual:** limpidity, transparency, colour, foam and effervescence. Beer should be clear
unless it is wheat, unfiltered or bottle-refermented; old beer turns opalescent. Foam left on
the glass is **Brussels lace** (*merletti di Bruxelles*). Lager and Pils want 2-3 inches of
lasting foam; English ales have little. Coarse bubbles can come from a rough glass.

**Nose:** hop, malt, fruit (**esters**, mostly high fermentation), caramel. Lager can smell
of cooked corn, ale of sugar and butter, dark beer of coffee and cocoa.

**Mouth:** sweetness from residual sugar; bitterness from hop resin, hop tannin and the husk;
**sourness from organic (lactic) acid** (Weizen, Belgian ale); salinity from the water; body
from extract.

| Beer | Serve at | Head |
|---|---|---|
| American lager | 5-7 °C | |
| Lager, Pilsner | 7-9 °C | Pilsner 3-4 in, over the rim |
| Weizen | 9-11 °C | **4-5 in** |
| Bock | 10-11 °C | |
| Lambic, Ale | 10-14 °C | Belgian ale 2-3 in; English about 1 in |
| Stout | 10-15 °C | about 2 in |
| Trappist, Abbey | **12-16 °C** | |

**Glasses:** the dimpled mug is Bavarian; a small tulip suits lager and pils, a large one
fruity ale, a flared tulip Belgian ale. Pot-belly glasses for ale and stout; the **coupe** for
Trappist and Abbey; the **pint (0.56 L)** for ale and stout; narrow glasses for lager and
pils; cylinders for Alt (tall) and Kölsch (short).

**Food:** pizza or panino is the classic. Light beer suits delicate food such as seafood;
dark beer suits grilled and roasted food. **Ales handle spicy food better than lager**,
removing more capsaicin. A fuller body suits meat.

## Making a distillate

The Arabs invented the **alembic**; alchemists and monks distilled oils and medicines from
1500 to 1600. Ageing in barrel came after Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy and Grappa. Northern
fruit gave Calvados (apple) and Slivovitz (plum). Cereals and potatoes gave whisky, gin and
vodka. Cane and molasses gave **rum**, agave **tequila and mezcal**, and maize and rye
**bourbon**, corn and rye whisky.

**Steps: must › fermentation › distillation › stabilisation › ageing.**

- **Must.** Fruit sugar just needs pressing. Starch needs an enzyme, either from the raw
  material itself or added. Ferment 3-4 days at 18-25 °C to 5-12% alcohol.
- **Distillation** separates by **boiling point**. **Discontinuous**: the boiler is refilled
  each batch. **Continuous**: fed through columns.
- **Stabilisation.** Lower the alcohol with deionised water, **chill to −10/−20 °C** so heavy
  molecules drop out, filter, and sometimes add sugar for roundness.
- **Ageing** in wood gives vanilla, spice, tobacco, dried fruit and honey by esterification.
  It depends on barrel size, wood origin and age, time, alcohol, temperature and humidity.
- **Aromatisation** is frowned on, except for gin, vodka and grappa.

**Components:** water 40-64%, sugar under 20 g/L, ethanol 36-60% (it sets the price).
**Methanol under 1%** of the alcohol. **Esters** give fruity and floral notes; **ketones**
give buttery ones; terpenes and pyrazines give primary fruit aromas.

**Tasting:** distillates should be brilliant, usually colourless, turning yellow with age.
Sip 2-5 ml. Use a **tulip glass of 100-120 ml**: Scotch's is more closed than Cognac's, and
Grappa's is the most open. No heating or dilution.

| Distillate | Serve at |
|---|---|
| Vodka, Aquavit | **0-4 °C** |
| Young fruit Aquavit | 6-8 °C |
| Young Grappa | 10-12 °C |
| Rum, blended or young whisky | 14-16 °C |
| Aged Aquavit, aged Grappa | 16-18 °C |
| Rum, malt or aged whisky, **Brandy, Cognac, Armagnac** | **18-20 °C** |

## Spirits of the world

| Spirit | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Whisky** | Ireland and Scotland; barley, maize, rye, oats, wheat. **Single Malt**: barley malt from one distillery. **Vatted / Pure Malt**: single malts from several. **Single Grain**: cereal whisky from one distillery. **Blended Scotch**: several. Blends need over half malt, aged 8-12 years. USA: **Bourbon (maize)**, Rye. Canada: rye blends. Japan: Scottish style |
| **Saké** | **not a distillate**: fermented malted rice; Japan, about 300 BC; about 1,800 producers, around Kyoto. **Moromi** is raw saké (about 20%). Served in the **sakazuki** cup from the **tokkuri** bottle, warmed to 40-50 °C. Commercial saké is about 16% |
| **Vodka** | from *voda / woda*, "water"; Russia and Poland claim it; rye and wheat; diluted to about 40%; chilled, with caviar and salmon |
| **Acquavite d'Uva** | Italian grape spirit; ferment at 18-22 °C; discontinuous or vacuum still, 65-70%; diluted to 40-45% |
| **Grappa** | from grape **pomace**; 70-86% off the still; single-variety if >85% of one grape; **vecchia** 1 year, **riserva / stravecchia** 18 months in oak |
| **Cognac** | north of Bordeaux; six zones (Grande Champagne › Bois Ordinaires); **Ugni Blanc 90%**, plus Folle Blanche, Colombard; **Alambic Charentais**, double distillation to 65-72%; **Limousin oak** |
| **Armagnac** | possibly the oldest (7th century); **Gascogne** (Bas Armagnac, Ténarèze, Haut Armagnac); mainly Ugni Blanc; **continuous Alambic Armagnaçais**, 58-63% |
| **Calvados** | **Normandy apple cider**; one continuous distillation; up to 15% pear; **at least 2 years** in oak |
| **Rum** | sugar cane, which Columbus brought to Spain; **agricultural** from cane juice (*vesou*), **industrial** from **molasses**; Blanco, Paille, Superior, Gold, Black, Añejo |
| **Tequila** | Mexico; **agave**; must is *aguamiel*, ferment is *pulque*; discontinuous still to 38-42%; Plata/Blanca; **Reposado 3-12 months**, Añejo ≥1 year, Muy Añejo 5-6 years |
