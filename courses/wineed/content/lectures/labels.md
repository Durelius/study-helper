## The seven main details

A label is called the **étiquette** in French and the **etichetta** in Italian. Details vary
by country, but these seven are usually there:

1. **Logo** of the producer
2. **Designation** of the wine
3. **Vintage** — the year of harvesting
4. **Bottling agency** and producer details
5. **Alcohol content**, in percent by volume (% vol)
6. **Volume** of wine (750 ml)
7. **Bottling number** or sequence

Beyond the designation, a producer can add a **commercial name** (a "fantasy" name such as
*Il Falco*). Other extras are descriptions of taste and aromas, an English translation, the
serving temperature and recommended food.

## Designation, vineyard and producer ranking

A **designation** identifies a wine by its **area of production together with its
winemaking technique and grape varietal**: *Barolo DOCG*, *Margaux AOC*. A producer who cannot
or will not follow a designation's rules can still label the wine Country wine or Table wine.
If the wine is still high quality, other classes exist for it, such as **VDQS** in France and
**IGP/IGT** in Italy.

- In **France**, producers inside one designation can be ranked further, for example **Premier
  Grand Cru Classé**. On *Château Margaux · Premier Grand Cru Classé · Margaux AOC*, the château
  is the producer, the ranking belongs to the producer, and *Margaux AOC* is the designation.
- In **Italy**, the vineyard can follow the designation: in **Barolo Bussia**, Barolo is the DOCG
  and Bussia is the vineyard.
- Some designations allow several grapes, so the **grape varietal** may be printed too.

## Ageing, alcohol and zone terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Riserva / Reserve** | aged in cask or barrel longer than the designation requires |
| **Superiore / Superior** | at least **0.5%** more alcohol than the designation's minimum |
| **Classico** | the historic production zone of an old designation (Chianti Classico, Soave Classico) |
| **Gran Selezione** | Chianti Classico from the estate's own grapes under stricter rules (since 2014) |

Chianti Classico needs at least 12% alcohol; **Riserva** needs at least **24 months'**
maturation (3 of them fining in bottle) and **12.5%**. Marsala is labelled by age: Fine
1 year, Superiore 2, **Superiore Riserva 4**, Vergine/Soleras 5, Stravecchio 10.

## Sparkling-wine labels

- **Blanc de Blancs** is Chardonnay only. **Blanc de Noirs** is Pinot Noir only.
- **Millésime** means more than 85% of the cuvée comes from one good year.
- Dosage (residual sugar, g/L):

| Pas dosé | Brut nature | Extra brut | Brut | Extra dry | Sec | Demi-sec | Doux |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 1 | < 3 | < 6 | < 15 | 12–20 | 18–35 | 33–50 | > 50 |

> **Exam focus.** *Extra dry* is **sweeter** than *Brut*, which is the commonest trap on this scale.

## Classification ladders

| | Highest | | | Lowest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **France** | AOC | Vin de Pays | Vin de France | |
| **Italy** | DOCG | DOC | IGT | VdT |

- **DOCG** has strict control *plus* a guarantee of quality.
- **DOC** is the largest class and usually medium quality.
- **IGT** is quality wine made outside the DOC rules; Super Tuscans such as Tignanello are *Rosso IGT*.
- **VdT** follows no specification.

**Bolgheri Sassicaia DOC** is the only designation tied to a single wine and producer.

**Bourgogne** ranks by terroir: **Grand cru** (about 2%, named without a village, e.g. Corton or
Montrachet) › **Premier cru** (about 12%, village + Premier cru) › **Village** › **Regional**.
Regional labels can be AOC Bourgogne, AOC + sub-zone (Mâcon-Villages) or AOC + grape
(Bourgogne Aligoté).
