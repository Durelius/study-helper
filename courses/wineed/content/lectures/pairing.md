## Wine balance

Taste-olfactive analysis weighs **softness** against **hardness**:

| Softness | Hardness |
|---|---|
| Sweetness | Acidity |
| Pseudo-warm sensation (alcohol) | Tannicity |
| Roundness (polyalcohol) | Saltiness |

A non-sweet red counts alcohol and polyalcohol as softness, and acidity, tannicity and
saltiness as hardness. A non-sweet white has **no tannicity**, because there is no tannin
from the skins. A balanced wine is mature; a quite balanced one leans young and acidic, or
soft. An unbalanced one is old or immature.

## The scales behind the scores

| Scale | Steps |
|---|---|
| Sweetness | dry 1-10 g/L › medium-dry 10-30 › medium-sweet 30-50 › sweet 50-100 › excessively sweet (a fault) |
| Pseudo-warm | light 4-7% › slightly warm 10-11% › moderately warm 11-12.5% › warm 12.5-15% › alcoholic >15% |
| Roundness | sharp › scarcely soft › quite soft › soft › velvety (high alcohol and polyalcohol; sweet wines) |
| Acidity | flat › scarcely fresh › quite fresh › fresh (young wine) › acidulous (too acidic) |
| Tannicity | flabby › scarcely tannic › quite tannic › tannic › astringent |
| Persistence | short <2 s › scarcely 2-4 s › quite 4-7 s › persistent 7-10 s › very persistent >10 s |

Cold suppresses sweetness but **raises tannicity**, so reds are not served chilled.

## The components of food

| Component | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Saltiness** | sodium chloride; a hardness | cured meat |
| **Bitter tendency**, **sour tendency** | scored as tendencies, since they are rarely strong | salad, tomato |
| **Sweetness** | sugar itself | desserts |
| **Sweet tendency** | natural sweetness of a savoury dish | egg, rice, pumpkin, seafood |
| **Fattiness** | solid fat that coats the tongue | cheese |
| **Oiliness** | liquid fat | olive oil |
| **Succulence** (juiciness) | juice released in the mouth | a semi-cooked steak |
| **Spiciness** | aroma and taste of spices | clove, cinnamon, black pepper |
| **Aromaticity** | basic aroma of the ingredients | olive oil, herbs |
| **T-O persistence** | flavour after swallowing, in seconds | |

## Opposite and parallel pairing

**Opposite pairing.** The wine cancels what the food has too much of:

- Saltiness, bitter and sour tendency (food hardness) → **softness** from polyalcohol.
- Spicy food, such as Thai, → a soft wine high in polyalcohol.
- Oiliness → **tannicity**, or enough alcohol if a red does not suit the dish.
- Succulence → **tannicity or alcohol**, which slow the salivary glands.
- Sweet tendency → adequate **acidity**.
- Solid fattiness → **acidity** (more saliva) or **effervescence** (CO2 cleans the tongue).

**Parallel pairing.** The wine matches the food:

- A dessert takes a wine with **the same sweetness**, so its sweetness is not reduced.
- Aromatic or spiced food takes a wine with **strong aroma**, so the aromas last longer.

## Drawing the polygon

Score every component from 0 to 10: imperceivable 0-2, slightly perceivable 2-4, moderately
4-6, perceivable 6-8, highly 8-10.

**Food, red pen.** Fattiness, sweet tendency, juiciness and oiliness each have their own
axis. Saltiness, bitter tendency, sour tendency and sweetness share one axis, and
persistence, spiciness and aromaticity share another. On a shared axis, plot the **highest**
score. Connect the marks.

**Wine, blue pen.** Alcoholicity and tannicity share an axis (tannicity is absent for a
white). Acidity, effervescence and saltiness share one. Sweetness and roundness share one,
and T-O intensity and persistence share one. Plot the leading score and connect the marks.

The sheet puts each wine axis **facing** the food it balances:

| Food axis | faces | Wine axis |
|---|---|---|
| sweet tendency, fattiness | → | acidity / effervescence / saltiness |
| juiciness, oiliness | → | alcoholicity / tannicity |
| saltiness / bitter / sour / sweetness | → | sweetness / roundness |
| persistence / spiciness / aromaticity | → | T-O intensity / persistence |

So to **complete a wine** for a drawn dish, give each wine axis the score of the leading food
component it faces. To **complete a dish** for a drawn wine, read it back the other way.

## Reading the result

| The two polygons | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Similar shape, not very wide, opposite peaks | Simple but compatible (opposite rule) |
| Similar shape, low scores on all axes | Simple but compatible |
| Similar shape, high scores on all axes | Very important, complement each other, well balanced |
| Different shape: food peaks, wine low everywhere | Simple but **incompatible**: the food has elements the wine cannot balance |
| Similar shape, wine larger | Wine **overshadows** food |
| Similar shape, food larger | Food **overshadows** wine |

The finished sheet also records the structure of the food (scarcely, moderately or fully
structured) and the body of the wine (weak, full, vigorous). The verdict is
**disharmonious**, **moderately harmonious** or **harmonious**.
