## Definitions and origin

**Enology** is the discipline of producing, conserving and maturing wine. A **sommelier** is
the trained professional of wine service and wine-and-food pairing. **Vitis silvestris**, the
oldest variety, goes back over 60 million years. **Vitis vinifera**, today's vine, is about
one million years old and is believed to come from South Asia. Wine vineyards appeared in
Eurasia around **5,000 BC**. In the late 1800s, **phylloxera**, a pest that attacks the roots,
killed nearly 90% of European vines. Vines today grow on resistant **American rootstocks**.

A vine bears no fruit for the first **1–3 years**, rises to normal yield by years **3–5**, and
declines at **25–40 years**. Each year it is productive from spring to late summer and
**dormant from autumn to late winter**.

## The grape and ripening

A berry is stalk, skin, seeds and pulp. The pulp has three layers: **acids nearest the seed,
sugar in the middle, tannins and aroma substances just under the skin**. The seed matters least
in winemaking, though it adds astringency.

As grapes ripen, **sugar rises and acid falls**. The two main acids are **malic and tartaric**.
Warm zones harvest early to keep acidity. **Anthocyanin**, the red skin pigment, *falls* after
ripening, while skin tannin rises a little. **Phenolic maturation** is phenolics concentrating in
skin and seeds.

**Terroir** combines climate (continental or coastal), topography (height, slope, the direction
the slope faces) and soil:

| Soil | Wine |
|---|---|
| Sandy | drains and holds heat → low acidity, low tannin, delicate aromas |
| Clayey | holds water, cooler → high body, extract, intense colour |
| Silty | holds water and heat → softness |
| Loamy | very fertile → tasteless, pale |
| Limy | calcium → high sugar → high alcohol |

## Types of wine

- **Red**: colour comes from **anthocyanin** in the skin. Most reds are still, but Lambrusco
  and Brachetto d'Acqui sparkle. **Carbonic maceration** gives the fruity *Vin Nouveau* style
  of Beaujolais.
- **White**: made from white *or red* grapes and needs no maceration. Whites usually keep less
  well than reds.
- **Rosé**: colour comes from **partial maceration**. It is closer in properties to white wine.
- **Sparkling**: the bubbles are **CO₂** from yeast, made in bottle (Méthode Champenoise) or in a
  tank (Metodo Martinotti/Charmat).
- **Dessert**: **Botrytis cinerea** (noble rot) dries grapes and concentrates sugar. Late harvest,
  dried grapes and **ice wine** (frozen water removed at pressing) do the same.
- **Fortified**: another alcohol is added, giving **15–22%** alcohol and at least ~50 g/L of sugar.
  The **Solera** system keeps quality consistent. Examples are Marsala (Italy), Sherry (Spain),
  Port and Madeira (Portugal).

## Vinification

The grapes are crushed and destemmed. In fermentation, yeast turns sugar into **ethyl alcohol +
CO₂** (plus glycerin, polyalcohol and acetaldehyde) at **15–30 °C**. Colour is extracted at the
same time, helped by **remontage**. During maturation, **Pediococcus** turns **malic → lactic**
acid, which softens the wine. Stainless steel keeps colour bright and fruit intense; oak gives
partial oxidation and more complex notes. Before filtration, **white yolk or bentonite** is added
to clarify. **SO₂** goes in before bottling against oxidation and bacteria. **Nitrogen** goes
into the bottle before corking. **Cork** lets some oxygen through, which suits long bottle ageing.
Silicone, plastic and screw caps keep oxygen out and suit wines to drink young.

## Sparkling methods

**Champagne** uses Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier. The same method is used for
Franciacorta, Trento and Cava. The steps:

1. The base blend is the **cuvée**.
2. **Liqueur de tirage** (wine, sugar and yeast) starts the second fermentation in a bottle
   closed with a crown cap.
3. **Remuage** tilts and turns the bottles to bring the yeast to the neck.
4. The frozen plug is removed **à la volée**.
5. **Liqueur d'expédition** is added.

The **Charmat** tank method was invented by Federico Martinotti and realised by Eugène Charmat.
It is faster than fermentation in bottle.

## Degustation and visual analysis

Degustation uses the **eyes, the nose and the tongue**. Before tasting:

- No strong perfume, strong food or smoking beforehand.
- No more than **10–12 samples** in a row.
- Don't judge a wine by its reputation.

Equipment:

- The old tool was the silver **tastevin**; today it is a thin, clear ISO glass, cleaned with
  **hot water and no detergent**.
- White glasses are smaller than red. Sweet wines get the smallest glasses. Wide glasses let
  important reds breathe.

Humans separate purple-red shades better than green-yellow shades.

**Visual analysis** covers limpidity, colour and consistency (swirl, watch the tears, called
**lacrima**). Sparkling wine is not swirled; its bubbles are judged instead.

- **Limpidity**: veiled (unacceptable) › quite limpid › limpid › crystal clear (whites, rosé) ›
  brilliant (sparkling).
- **Consistency**: flowing (unacceptable) › scarcely consistent › quite consistent (tears far
  apart) › consistent (tears close, high alcohol) › oily (sweet).
- **Red colour**: purple red (young) › ruby (most) › garnet (aged) › orange red (old).
- **White colour**: greenish yellow (young) › straw › golden (aged) › amber (dessert, fortified).
- **Rosé colour**: light coppery pink (shortest maceration) › salmon › cherry red › dark pink.
- A continuous stream of bubbles is **perlage**.

## Olfactory analysis: the aromas

Smell works by two routes. **Ortho-nasal** is direct inhalation. **Retro-nasal** is the exhaled
path, which is key in the taste-olfactory phase. The tasting goes in three steps:

1. A first sniff, to note the aromas and their intensity.
2. A swirl, to oxygenate the wine.
3. A long second sniff, to judge complexity and quality.

| Stage | Origin | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | the grape variety, **terpenes** in the skin | aromatic grapes: Gewürztraminer, Malvasia, Muscat, Brachetto |
| **Secondary** | pressing, fermentation, early maturation | malolactic softening; esters; the **vinous** note |
| **Tertiary** | long maturation; **esterification** with partial oxidation | vinous is gone; primary remain |

Aroma families:

- **Floral**: acacia, violet
- **Fruity**: citrus, berries
- **Grassy and vegetal**: fern, truffle
- **Mineral**: petroleum, flint
- **Spicy**: clove, pepper
- **Toast**: coffee, cacao
- **Animal**: leather, fur
- **Ethereal**: wax, acetone

The three scales:

- **Intensity**: lacking (unacceptable) › scarcely intense › quite intense › intense › very intense.
- **Complexity**: lacking › scarcely complex (doesn't evolve) › quite complex (changes within few
  families) › complex (many families) › ample (always changing).
- **Quality**: coarse and scarcely fine (both unacceptable) › quite fine › fine › excellent.

**Serving temperatures:**

| Wine | °C |
|---|---|
| Sparkling | 6–8 |
| Young fruity white | 8–10 |
| Aromatic white, rosé, dessert white | 10–12 |
| Aged white | 12–14 |
| Fruity red | 14–16 |
| Bodied red | 16–18 |
| Long-aged tannic red | 18–20 |

Cold suppresses sweetness and **increases tannin**, so reds are not served chilled.
