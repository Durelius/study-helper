## History and legislation in brief

The first vines were planted at **Narbonne around 600 BC**. From there viticulture spread west
along the Garonne to Bordeaux and north up the Rhône, then into the Loire and Champagne, carried
by the Church after Rome fell. The **1855 classification** reflected Bordeaux's boom. Oidium,
mildew and **phylloxera** then destroyed most vines. In **1930** the **AOC** system was set up to
control quality.

There are three levels: **AOC** (strictest) › **Vin de Pays** › **Vin de France** (base). France
has about 900,000 ha of vines: 55% AOC, 31% Vin de Pays, 14% Vin de Table.

## Bordeaux

Bordeaux makes mainly reds from **Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Merlot**, plus the
sweet white **Sauternes**. The **Garonne** and **Dordogne** divide it into three zones.

| Zone | Soil | Leading grape | AOCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Left bank** (Médoc) | gravel | **Cabernet Sauvignon** | Saint-Estèphe (also limestone, more Merlot), **Pauillac** (gravel, masculine), Saint-Julien (milder), **Margaux** (clay, soft, feminine) |
| **Right bank** | limestone + clay | **Merlot** | **Saint-Émilion** (with Cabernet Franc), **Pomerol** (no limestone, velvety; Pétrus, Le Pin) |
| **Entre-Deux-Mers** | | Merlot (Clairet) | AOC Bordeaux, Bordeaux Supérieur (higher alcohol) |
| **Sauternes, Barsac** | | **Sémillon** + Sauvignon Blanc, botrytised | Château d'Yquem: *Premier cru supérieur* |

The 1855 classification ranked 60 Médoc and Graves producers into **five** levels. The five red
**Premiers crus** are Haut-Brion, Lafite-Rothschild, Latour, Margaux and Mouton Rothschild.

## Bourgogne and Beaujolais

The grapes are **Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Gamay**. The soils are limestone with clay, and the
climate is continental.

- **Chablis**: 100% Chardonnay; high acid, mineral. Four levels: Petit Chablis › Chablis ›
  Premier Cru › Grand Cru (about 3%).
- **Côte de Nuits**: the world's key **Pinot Noir** zone (Gevrey-Chambertin, Morey-St-Denis,
  Chambolle-Musigny).
- **Côte de Beaune**: famous for **Chardonnay** whites (Meursault, Puligny-/Chassagne-Montrachet),
  plus reds such as Pommard and Volnay.
- **Côte Chalonnaise**: Pinot Noir and Chardonnay; **Bouzeron** is the birthplace of **Aligoté**.
- **Mâconnais**: Gamay reds and Chardonnay; **Pouilly-Fuissé** is its star (Chardonnay).
- **Beaujolais**: granite; **Gamay**; *Beaujolais Nouveau* by carbonic maceration.

## Champagne

The soil is chalk and the climate is cool and windy. Champagne is made by **Méthode
Traditionnelle**, and its founding story is Dom Pérignon's. The grapes are **Chardonnay, Pinot
Noir and Pinot Meunier**, and each zone has its specialty:

| Zone | Grape |
|---|---|
| Montagne de Reims | Pinot Noir |
| Vallée de la Marne | **Pinot Meunier** (buds late, ripens early) |
| Côte des Blancs, Côte de Sézanne | Chardonnay |
| Côte des Bar | Pinot Noir |

## Vallée de la Loire

More than half of the Loire's wine is white.

| Sub-region | Grape | AOC |
|---|---|---|
| Pays Nantais | **Muscadet** | Muscadet (seafood, mussels) |
| Anjou-Saumur | **Chenin Blanc** | Savennières (slate, mineral) |
| Touraine | **Cabernet Franc** reds; Chenin Blanc | Chinon, Bourgueil; **Vouvray** (sec to sparkling) |
| Central Loire | **Sauvignon Blanc** | **Sancerre** (more body and fruit), **Pouilly-Fumé** (flint) |

> **Exam focus.** *Pouilly-Fumé* is Loire Sauvignon Blanc. *Pouilly-Fuissé* is Mâconnais
> Chardonnay.

## Vallée du Rhône

- **North**: continental climate. Reds are **Syrah only**: Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage,
  Crozes-Hermitage. Whites are **Viognier** (Condrieu, Château-Grillet) and Marsanne/Roussanne.
- **South**: Mediterranean climate; full-bodied, high-alcohol wines from **Grenache**, Syrah,
  Cinsaut, Carignan and Mourvèdre. **Châteauneuf-du-Pape** allows up to **13** red varieties.
  Gigondas and Vacqueyras sit on limestone. The whites are Viognier, Marsanne and Roussanne.
- **GSM** (Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre) is the classic southern French blend.

## Other zones

| Zone | Grapes and wines |
|---|---|
| **Alsace** | Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris, Sylvaner, Muscat; Vendange Tardive, SGN; Crémant d'Alsace |
| **Languedoc-Roussillon** | about 90% red from Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre, Carignan; Vin de Pays with international grapes |
| **Provence** | France's **rosé** source; Côtes de Provence (Cinsaut, Grenache); **Bandol = Mourvèdre** |
| **Corse** | white **Vermentino**; AOC Ajaccio from native **Sciacarello** |
| **Sud-Ouest** | **Madiran = Tannat**; **Cahors = Malbec** (Auxerrois); Bergerac, Monbazillac; Jurançon |
